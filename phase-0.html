The 6 Non-Negotiable Rules

These rules cannot be broken — they decide if your house lasts 30 or 100 years

READ FIRST
▼
What is a Non-Negotiable?These 6 rules cannot be skipped or reduced, no matter what the contractor says or what budget pressure there is. They decide if your house lasts 30 years or 100 years.
#The RuleWhy It Cannot Be Skipped
1Earthquake Safety FirstNepal is in Seismic Zone V (highest risk). Structure must be earthquake-safe first. Beautiful look comes second.
2Foundation for G+3, Build G+2Designing foundation for one extra floor means: stronger columns, longer life, better earthquake performance, ability to add floor in future.
3Waterproofing is Invisible LuxuryNobody sees it. But every monsoon for 50 years you will be thankful. Most house problems after 10–15 years come from water, not design.
4Service Zones — All Bathrooms StackedAll bathrooms on every floor must be directly above each other (same location). This groups all pipes in one shaft, making repairs simple without breaking multiple walls.
5Ground Floor Designed for Old AgeYour parents live there now. You will be old in 30 years. Wide doors, no level differences, anti-skid floors. Costs nothing extra now. Impossible to change later.
6Ventilation + Dust ControlPokhara has humidity and dust. Every room needs openings on two walls (cross-ventilation). Mosquito mesh on all windows. All frames must seal properly to keep dust out. Exhaust fans in kitchen and bathrooms are mandatory.

Who to Hire and in What Order

Hire these professionals in this exact sequence

6 Professionals
▼
⚠️ Most Common Mistake: People hire the architect first. Wrong. In Nepal's earthquake zone, hire: Soil Engineer → Structural Engineer → Architect. Beauty comes after safety.
OrderWho to HireWhat They DoHow to Verify
1stSoil Test CompanyDrills into your land and tests what kind of soil is below. Tells you how deep to build and what type of foundation. Like an MRI scan before surgery.They must physically drill with a machine. You must be present. Ask for past reports from Pokhara area.
2ndStructural EngineerDesigns the skeleton — columns, beams, slabs. Decides steel quantity, concrete grade, foundation type. Most important professional for safety in Nepal.Must be registered with Nepal Engineering Council (NEC). Ask for their NEC number and verify it.
3rdArchitectDesigns layout and look — floor plan, rooms, doors, windows, appearance. Works together with structural engineer.Ask to see real photos of houses they have built (not computer renders). Visit one past project in person.
4thContractorThe team that actually builds the house. Masons, helpers, equipment.Visit at least 2 previous houses they built. Talk to homeowners without contractor present. Written contract only.
5thPlumberInstalls all water pipes — supply, drainage, hot and cold water.Ask if they will draw a full pipe layout before starting. Ask about P-traps (explained in Phase 3).
5thElectricianInstalls all wiring, switches, sockets, protection devices.Ask for a room-by-room wiring layout before starting. Check they use ISI-marked copper wire.
🚨 Immediate Red Flag: Any contractor who says "soil test is a waste of money" — stop talking to them immediately. This means they want to skip engineering so they can cut corners.

Smart Planning — Things Most People Miss

Critical decisions before touching a single brick

Extra Tips
▼
💡 Smart TipStart construction between October and February in Pokhara. Avoid June–September (monsoon). Never lay foundation during monsoon — rain floods pits, weakens soil.
⚠️ ImportantAlways keep a 15–20% extra budget beyond your estimate. Construction in Nepal almost always costs more than planned due to material price changes and unexpected issues.
💡 Smart TipRegister your building plan with the local municipality (Nagarpalika) BEFORE starting. Building without approval = demolition order possible. Municipal stamp on your drawings is mandatory.
✅ Do ThisGet COPIES of every document from every professional before making final payment. Soil test report, structural drawings, signed contract — everything. Keep a folder.
⚠️ ImportantNever share ownership of land with multiple people before construction. Unclear ownership causes construction to stop legally. Make sure Lal Purja is in one clear name.
💡 Smart TipIf you plan to take a bank loan, start the process 3–4 months before construction. Banks are slow. Construction cannot wait for banks.
✅ Do ThisTake out construction insurance. It covers: worker injury, theft of materials, fire, natural disaster damage during construction. Very cheap compared to cost of a claim.

Money and Legal — Before Touching a Single Brick

The financial and legal decisions that determine success or failure

Must Read
▼
⚠️ Critical Most first-time homebuilders in Nepal go over budget by 30–40% and regret many decisions. The root cause is almost always: started construction without a proper plan, contract, or budget.

BOQ — Bill of Quantities (Most Important Document Nobody Talks About)

💡 Know This A BOQ is a detailed list of EVERY material needed to build your house — with quantities, unit rates, and total cost. Example: "RCC M25 concrete for columns — 12 cubic metres @ NPR 8,500/m³ = NPR 102,000". A BOQ for your house covers 50–100 line items.
✅ Do This Ask your structural engineer or a quantity surveyor to prepare a BOQ before signing any contract. Cost: NPR 10,000–25,000. This one document lets you compare quotes from contractors fairly, track every rupee spent, and immediately detect if a contractor is cutting corners on material quantities.
⚠️ Critical Without a BOQ, a contractor can reduce steel from 100kg per cubic metre to 70kg. You cannot detect this. With a BOQ, you know exactly how much steel should be used and can weigh/check delivery quantities.

Neighbour NOC (No Objection Certificate)

⚠️ Critical In Nepal, if you build a wall on or near a shared boundary, you must get written NOC from the neighbour. Without this, they can file a complaint that stops construction legally — even after you have spent millions. Get this BEFORE foundation.
✅ Do This Visit both neighbours, explain your plan, show them your building line, and get a signed letter saying they have no objection to your construction. Keep original copies. This takes one afternoon but can prevent months of legal delays.

Temporary Electricity and Water for Construction

💡 Know This Construction needs electricity (for power tools, lighting, welding) and water (for concrete mixing, curing, cleaning). Arrange a temporary connection or agreement with neighbours before starting. Without this, workers use improvised solutions — like adding less water to concrete because there is not enough supply — which compromises quality.

Plinth Height — Flood and Damp Protection

⚠️ Critical Plinth height = how high your ground floor sits above the natural ground or road level. Minimum 600mm (2 feet) above road level for Pokhara. Many contractors build at road level to save material. Then: rain splash damages the wall base, mosquitoes breed in dampness, and road resurfacing (which raises road level over years) eventually puts your ground floor below road level causing flooding.
✅ Do This Tell your engineer and contractor: "Plinth height minimum 600mm above road level." This costs very little extra but protects the house permanently.
