Anti-Skid Rating for Bathroom Floors

🚨 Most bathroom accidents are caused by slippery tiles. You asked specifically: no slipping barefoot or with slippers.
RatingWhat It MeansUse Where
R9Slightly rough. Safe when damp.Dry bathroom areas, corridor
R10Noticeably rough. Safe when moderately wet.✅ Bathroom floor (minimum acceptable)
R11Very rough. Safe in standing water.✅ Shower area floor (recommended here)
R12Maximum roughness. Industrial level.Outdoor, parking in rain
✅ Tell the tile shopkeeper: "I need tiles with R10 or R11 anti-skid rating for bathroom floors." Ask them to show you the tile specification sheet with this rating number printed on it.

How to Make Sure Bathroom Floor Slopes Correctly

Required slope: 1 centimetre drop per 100 centimetres of floor (1:100 ratio)

How to check during and after tile laying:
1. After tiles are laid (before grout is filled), pour a mug of water anywhere on the bathroom floor
2. The water must immediately start flowing toward the drain
3. No water should pool anywhere on the floor
4. If water pools: that area is flat or wrong slope — tell the mason to redo it before grouting

Simple field check: Place a marble or small ball on any part of the bathroom floor. It must roll toward the drain. If it stays still or rolls away from drain, the slope is wrong.

✅ Tell the tile mason BEFORE he starts: "The floor must slope 1 cm per meter toward the drain. We will test with water before you apply grout."
