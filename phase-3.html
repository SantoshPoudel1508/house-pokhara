P-Trap — What It Is and Why Every Drain Needs One

The water seal that stops sewer smell entering your rooms

Must Know
▼
The U-shaped bend always holds water. This water acts as a seal that stops bad-smelling and harmful sewer gas from coming back up through the drain into the room.
P-Trap Simply ExplainedImagine a straw bent into a U-shape at the bottom. Water stays in the bend. If you blow air from below, the water blocks it. A P-trap does the same thing — the water seal stops sewer gas from entering your bathroom.

✅ Every floor drain, wash basin drain, and kitchen sink drain MUST have a P-trap.
✅ If a bathroom is not used for weeks, pour water down each drain to refill the trap.
❌ Without P-trap: your bathroom will smell like sewer even when everything else is clean.

Complete Plumbing System — Hot, Cold, Tanks, Rules

Every pipe, every fitting, tank placement

Full Detail
▼
Water Tank Placement
Underground Sump Tank: REAR of house. You already have a water passage at the rear — connect here. Keeps front clean and visible.
Overhead Tank: CENTRE or REAR of roof. Central position gives equal pressure to all bathrooms.
Sewage: REAR. Connect all drains to the rear passage drainage system. Keep the front completely clean — no tanks or pipes visible from road.

Important Rules for Every Plumber

  • P-trap at every drain: Every floor drain, basin, kitchen sink — no exceptions
  • Service zones: All bathrooms stacked vertically on every floor (same position)
  • Stopcock (shut-off tap) at every bathroom branch — so one bathroom can be shut off without affecting the whole house
  • Drain slope: All drain pipes must slope at 1 cm per meter toward drain — test with water before closing walls
  • Hot water pipe insulation: Wrap all hot water pipes with foam sleeve to reduce heat loss
  • AC drain pipe: Each AC unit must have a dedicated drain pipe — not left to drip down the wall
