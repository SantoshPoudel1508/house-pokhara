P-Trap — What It Is and Why Every Drain Needs One

The water seal that stops sewer smell entering your rooms

Must Know
▼
The U-shaped bend always holds water. This water acts as a seal that stops bad-smelling and harmful sewer gas from coming back up through the drain into the room.
P-Trap Simply ExplainedImagine a straw bent into a U-shape at the bottom. Water stays in the bend. If you blow air from below, the water blocks it. A P-trap does the same thing — the water seal stops sewer gas from entering your bathroom.

✅ Every floor drain, wash basin drain, and kitchen sink drain MUST have a P-trap.
✅ If a bathroom is not used for weeks, pour water down each drain to refill the trap.
❌ Without P-trap: your bathroom will smell like sewer even when everything else is clean.

Important Rules for Every Plumber

  • P-trap at every drain: Every floor drain, basin, kitchen sink — no exceptions
  • Service zones: All bathrooms stacked vertically on every floor (same position)
  • Stopcock (shut-off tap) at every bathroom branch — so one bathroom can be shut off without affecting the whole house
  • Drain slope: All drain pipes must slope at 1 cm per meter toward drain — test with water before closing walls
  • Hot water pipe insulation: Wrap all hot water pipes with foam sleeve to reduce heat loss
  • AC drain pipe: Each AC unit must have a dedicated drain pipe — not left to drip down the wall

Smart Plumbing — Details That Save Repairs

Professional plumber tricks for 30-year problem-free plumbing

Extra Tips
▼
💡 Smart TipMark the exact location of every embedded pipe on a sketch/photo BEFORE plastering walls. When you need to drill a screw 5 years later, you will know exactly where pipes are and avoid puncturing them.
✅ Do ThisGas pipe planning: decide NOW whether you will use LPG cylinder (bottle) or piped gas. If cylinder, plan the cylinder storage area (outside/well-ventilated). If piped gas in future, run an empty conduit now.
⚠️ ImportantGrease trap: kitchen drain water contains oil and grease that solidifies in pipes over years and blocks them. Install a small grease trap (interceptor) in the kitchen drain line before it connects to the main drain. Cleaning every 6 months prevents permanent blockage.
💡 Smart TipHot water pipe insulation in Pokhara winters: wrap ALL hot water pipes with foam lagging (pipe insulation sleeve). Without it, you wait 5 minutes for hot water while the cold water sitting in the pipe drains away. With insulation, hot water arrives in 30 seconds.
✅ Do ThisProvide an access panel (small removable section in wall or floor) at every major plumbing junction — where pipes meet, where valves are. This 30-minute extra work during construction saves wall-breaking later for every repair.
⚠️ ImportantNever use plastic flexible hose (the silver braided type) for permanent connections inside walls. Use only rigid CPVC. Flexible hoses split within 5–7 years. Use flexible hose only for the final short connection from wall to taps/toilet.

Plumbing — Sizing and System Design

Get the sizes right or you will have poor water pressure and constant blockages

Must Read

Water Tank Sizing — How Much You Actually Need

⚠️ Critical Most first-time builders install tanks that are too small. Then: water runs out daily, pump runs continuously, pressure is low during peak morning usage. Size your tanks correctly from the start — changing tank size later means breaking construction.
TankRecommended SizeMinimum SizeCalculation Basis
Underground sump12,000–15,000 litres8,000 litres3 days storage for family of 6
Overhead tank3,000 litres2,000 litres1 day peak usage buffer
Total water stored15,000–18,000 litres10,000 litresCritical for Pokhara supply interruptions
💡 Know This Pokhara municipal water supply is intermittent — sometimes off for 2–3 days. With 15,000 litres stored, your family can live normally for 5–7 days without any municipal supply.

Vent Pipe — The Overlooked Part of Drainage

⚠️ Critical Every drain system needs a vent pipe — a pipe that rises from the drainage system up through the roof and opens to outside air. Without a vent: when water flows down a drain, it creates a vacuum that sucks the water OUT of every P-trap it passes. All your P-traps fail simultaneously. Every drain in the house smells like sewer.
✅ Do This Ask your plumber: "Where is the vent pipe on your drainage plan?" A proper drainage design has one main vent pipe per floor (typically 50mm diameter, rises through walls or externally and opens 500mm above roof level). If your plumber does not know what a vent pipe is, get a different plumber.

Rainwater and Sewage — Must Be Completely Separate

⚠️ Critical In Nepal, many builders connect roof rainwater drain pipes to the sewage system. This is illegal, causes sewage backup into bathrooms during heavy rain, and overloads the municipal sewer. Roof rainwater = clean. It must go to: your underground sump tank (for reuse) OR directly to a soakpit OR to the road storm drain — NEVER to the sewage system.

Hard Water — Test and Plan Before Installing Pipes

💡 Know This Before finalising your plumbing plan, test your municipal water for TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) and hardness. In many areas of Pokhara, hardness is 200–400 ppm — moderate to hard. Above 200 ppm: scale builds inside pipes and geysers, white marks on all taps, poor soap lather. Ask your plumber to send a water sample to a lab (NPR 500–1,000).
✅ Do This If TDS > 300 or hardness > 200 ppm: install a whole-house water softener on the main supply line before it splits into hot and cold. This single installation prevents: geyser failure (scale inside), blocked tap aerators, white tile stains, dry skin. Return on investment within 3 years.

Where to Place Your Water Sump and Septic Tank

The most important site planning decision — get this wrong and it costs you forever

Critical Planning
▼
⚠️ Never Place Both Tanks at the Rear Your rear soil is already soft from the drainage passage. Adding a water sump AND septic tank at the rear creates three serious problems: (1) Chronically waterlogged soil weakens your rear house foundation. (2) Waterlogged soil can liquefy during an earthquake — a main cause of house collapse. (3) Only 2–3 metres separation between clean water and sewage is dangerously close in earthquake country.

The Correct Layout — Maximum Separation

Water sump at FRONT (clean, dry soil). Septic at REAR (connects to passage). 18 metres separation = zero contamination risk.

Septic Tank — Rear Placement (Properly Built for Soft Soil)

⚠️ Rear Soil Is Already Soft The drainage passage keeps the rear soil permanently moist. Your septic tank MUST be built to handle this — standard septic tank construction is NOT enough here.
DetailSpecification for Your Soft Soil Condition
LocationRear right corner, minimum 1.5m from rear boundary, minimum 1.5m from house rear wall
Base slab200mm thick RCC base slab extending 300mm beyond tank walls on all sides — distributes load to prevent tank sinking in soft soil
Walls150mm thick RCC, M25 concrete — stronger than standard to handle soil pressure from soft/wet ground
WaterproofingBoth inside AND outside must be waterproofed — outside waterproofing prevents sewage seeping into soft soil and migrating toward house foundation
TypeTwo-chamber design: Chamber 1 (1.5m×1m) for solids settling. Chamber 2 (1m×1m) for liquid treatment. Total footprint 2.5m×1m — fits in 5ft rear setback.
OutletConnect overflow pipe directly to rear drainage passage — eliminates need for soak pit (which would further saturate already soft soil)
MaintenanceDesludge every 5–7 years. Flexible hose from desludging vehicle reaches from road in front (hoses are 50m+ long) — no vehicle access to rear needed.
