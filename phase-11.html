No Sharp Edges — Every Location in the House

LocationSolutionWhen
All wall corners (entire house)"Bullnose corner bead" — a rounded metal strip on every internal corner during plastering. Creates ~12mm radius curve instead of sharp 90-degree corner.During plastering — cannot change after
Kitchen counter edgesEased or pencil round edge profile on all countertop edgesDuring countertop order
Cabinet door and drawer edgesRounded 2mm PVC edge banding on all exposed edgesDuring cabinet manufacturing
Staircase step front edgeRounded nosing profile + anti-skid strip on every step edgeDuring staircase construction
Staircase handrail endsSmooth return cap (curved end piece) at top and bottom of every handrail — prevents protruding end from poking eyes of childrenDuring staircase installation
Window sill corners5–10mm chamfer (angled cut) on all four corners of granite sill — quarry does this if askedDuring stone cutting
All furnitureSpecify "no sharp edges, radius corner" to every furniture supplier and carpenterWhen ordering
TV unit and shelvingAll shelf edges rounded — same PVC edge banding as kitchen cabinetsDuring carpentry work

Music and Hobby Room — Plan Now, Build Later

Smart Approach: The expensive part (soundproofing treatment) can be added later when you are ready. But some things are much cheaper to do NOW during construction.

Do NOW (during construction — very low extra cost):
1. Designate one room on the first floor for this purpose
2. Use thicker walls for that room: 230mm wall (two bricks thick) instead of standard 115mm — doubles sound insulation
3. Install conduit pipes in ceiling and walls for future audio cables and speaker wires
4. Plan 6 power sockets in this room and a dedicated AC point
5. Embed small steel plates in ceiling for future speaker mounting

Do LATER (when ready):
• Acoustic foam panels on walls
• Bass trap panels in corners
• Thick carpet on floor
• Heavy curtains on window
• Door with rubber gasket seal

Smart Interiors — Move In Ready

Lessons from people who have lived in new houses

Extra Tips
▼
💡 Smart TipMove into the house for 3 MONTHS before buying all final furniture. You will discover which corners are unused, where natural light actually falls, where you actually sit, and what storage you actually need. Most people who furnish immediately regret several purchases.
⚠️ ImportantAir conditioner placement: do NOT install AC directly above where people sit, sleep, or eat. Cold air blowing directly on a person for hours causes health problems. Mount AC on the wall so that cold air blows along the length of the room, not directly down on furniture.
✅ Do ThisFalse ceiling: only install where genuinely needed — to hide visible pipes, ugly beam soffits, or wire clusters. A full false ceiling throughout the house reduces room height, reduces ventilation, and adds significant cost. Minimal use is smarter.
💡 Smart TipFurniture buying sequence: Buy in this order for best results: 1) Essential beds and dining first (so you can live). 2) Wait 2 months. 3) Kitchen cabinetry. 4) Wait 1 month. 5) Sofas and storage. 6) Decorative items last — buy these over years, not all at once.
⚠️ ImportantStorage for important documents: install a small hidden safe (wall safe or floor safe) in the master bedroom or study. Store: Lal Purja original, passport, birth certificates, property documents. Do not keep originals in an easily accessible place.
✅ Do ThisInternet/data points: before closing walls, install Cat6 data cable to every room — living room (2 points), bedrooms (1 each), study (2 points), and one outdoor point for potential CCTV or gate camera. Wireless is convenient but wired internet is 10x more reliable for working from home.

Snag List — The Professional Handover Process

💡 Know This A "snag list" (also called a defect list or punch list) is a written document listing EVERY imperfection, incomplete item, or quality issue found in the house before final payment is made to the contractor. This is standard practice in professional construction worldwide but almost nobody does it in Nepal.
✅ Do This Before making your final payment to any contractor, walk through every single room systematically and write down every issue. Give the contractor this written list and pay the final amount only after EVERY item is fixed to your satisfaction. Hold back minimum 10% of the contract value as retention until all snags are resolved.

What to Check on Your Snag Walk

AreaWhat to Check
Every roomAll walls plumb (use spirit level), no cracks, paint uniform, no brush marks
All floorsNo hollow tiles (tap test), grouting complete, no lippage (tiles at different heights)
All doorsOpens/closes without sticking, locks work, door stop prevents wall damage
All windowsOpen/close smoothly, locks engage, no gaps when closed, mesh intact
All bathroomsDrains flow, no leaks at joints, shower pressure adequate, exhaust fan works
ElectricalEvery switch and socket tested, all MCBs labelled, earthing tested
PlumbingAll taps open fully, no leaks under sinks, water pressure adequate at all outlets
ExternalAll drains clear, compound wall complete, gate opens smoothly, no exposed wire ends

Documents to Collect Before Final Payment

⚠️ Critical Before paying the final bill to your contractor, ensure you have received all of these:
  • Signed completion certificate from your structural engineer
  • All concrete cube test reports from the lab
  • Electrical earthing test report (from electrician)
  • Plumbing pressure test report
  • Waterproofing flood test photograph/report
  • Warranty letter from contractor (minimum 1 year for structural defects, 6 months for finishing)
  • All material certificates (steel MTC, cement quality certificates)
  • Keys to all doors, windows, lock combinations
  • All appliance manuals and warranty cards
  • Contact numbers of plumber and electrician who did the work
✅ Do This These documents are your legal protection. If a structural crack appears in Year 3, your contractor says "not my problem." With a signed completion certificate and concrete cube test reports showing correct quality, you have grounds to demand they fix it. Without documents, you have nothing.

One Year Defect Liability Period

💡 Know This Standard in construction contracts worldwide: the contractor is responsible for fixing defects that appear within 1 year of handover at no cost to you. This is called the Defect Liability Period (DLP). Include this clause in your contract and keep the contractor's contact details. Within the first monsoon after moving in, check all waterproofing carefully — if any leakage appears, it must be fixed under DLP.
