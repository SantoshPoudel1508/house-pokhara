Waterproofing — Every Location, Flood Test, Why Terrace Tiles

Done right once. Fail this and you fix it every monsoon forever.

Non-Negotiable
▼

Why You Need Terrace Tiles (Many People Ask This)

The waterproofing membrane on your roof is like a plastic film — it cannot survive direct sunlight and foot traffic.

Without terrace tiles:
• Sunlight (UV rays) breaks down the waterproofing membrane in 2–3 years
• Walking on the roof scratches and punctures it
• It cracks from heat expansion and contraction every season
→ Result: roof leaking into the room below within 3–5 years

With terrace tiles:
• Tiles protect the membrane from sun, footsteps, and weather
• Light-coloured tiles reflect heat — room below stays cooler
• Membrane lasts 15–20 years

✅ Use light-coloured (white or light grey) non-polished terrace tiles. Light = reflects heat. Dark = absorbs heat, heats the room below.

The Flood Test — How It Works

After applying membrane on terrace:
1. Create a temporary dam around the terrace drain area
2. Fill the terrace with water to 50mm (2 inches) depth
3. Leave for 48 hours — do not remove water
4. Go to the room below and check the ceiling for any dampness or water marks
5. If no dampness: waterproofing has passed — proceed to lay tiles
6. If any dampness: fix the membrane, redo the test

✅ Make this a milestone — no payment for waterproofing until flood test passes with you present.

Smart Waterproofing — Common Failures

Why waterproofing fails and how to prevent it

Extra Tips
▼
⚠️ ImportantApply waterproofing membrane only when temperature is between 10°C and 35°C. In Pokhara winter (December–February), night temperatures drop below 10°C. Cold temperatures prevent membrane from curing correctly and it will fail in the first monsoon.
💡 Smart TipParapet wall (the low wall at the edge of your terrace) is the most common failure point. Water collects at the parapet base. The waterproofing membrane MUST go up the parapet wall minimum 300mm (1 foot) above the terrace level.
✅ Do ThisBefore applying any waterproofing, fill ALL cracks in the concrete slab with polymer-modified repair mortar. Even hairline cracks. Waterproofing membrane cannot bridge cracks — it tears there first.
⚠️ ImportantExpansion joint: if your terrace is longer than 6 metres, it needs an expansion joint (a controlled crack line with flexible filler). Without it, the slab cracks randomly from thermal expansion and tears the waterproofing.
💡 Smart TipTest all terrace drain outlets BEFORE waterproofing begins. Pour water down each drain and confirm it flows freely. A blocked drain after waterproofing means water pools and finds the weakest point.
✅ Do ThisAfter the flood test passes and tiles are laid, check the underside of the slab (the ceiling of the room below) once more after the first heavy monsoon rain. A small damp patch is easier to fix early than after years of water damage.

Waterproofing — The Hidden Failures

90% of waterproofing failures happen at joints and transitions — not on flat surfaces

Must Read
▼

Sump Tank Waterproofing — Often Forgotten

⚠️ Critical Your underground sump tank stores water you DRINK. If the tank is not properly waterproofed inside AND outside, two problems occur: 1) Groundwater seeps IN, contaminating your drinking water with soil bacteria and chemicals. 2) Water seeps OUT, wasting money and damaging the soil structure around your foundation.
✅ Do This Specify: apply crystalline waterproofing compound to the concrete mix when casting the sump tank. After curing, apply 2 coats of food-safe waterproof coating on the INSIDE of the tank (Sika Monotop or similar food-safe product). This is different from roof waterproofing — must be food-safe. Fit a manhole cover with mosquito-proof mesh to prevent dengue mosquito breeding.

Wall-to-Floor Junction — The Most Common Leak Point

💡 Know This In bathrooms and kitchen, the joint where the wall meets the floor is the highest-risk point for water leakage. Water seeps through this joint and damages the wall below and the room below. Most waterproofing failures in bathrooms start at corners, not the flat floor.
✅ Do This Specify to waterproofing contractor: apply a cove fillet (a rounded mortar fill in all corners where wall meets floor, creating a curved rather than sharp 90-degree corner) BEFORE applying waterproofing membrane. Then apply the membrane continuously from floor up the wall and into the cove. Never apply membrane in a sharp corner — it tears there from movement.

Compound and Boundary Wall Waterproofing

💡 Know This Your compound/boundary wall is exposed to rain on both sides. Without a proper coping (top cap) on the wall, water enters the top of the wall, saturates it, causes white salt stains (efflorescence), and eventually crumbles the wall from inside.
✅ Do This Specify: cast a continuous sloped RCC coping (cap) on top of the entire compound wall, minimum 75mm thick, sloped on both sides to shed water. Apply waterproof mortar for the coping. This extends compound wall life from 10 years to 50+ years.

Efflorescence — White Salt Marks on Walls

💡 Know This Efflorescence = white chalky deposits on the outside of walls. Cause: water soaks through the wall, picks up mineral salts from cement and brick, and deposits them when it evaporates on the surface. It looks ugly and indicates ongoing water infiltration.
⚠️ Critical Prevention is far easier than cure. Prevention: correct waterproofing, waterproof plaster, and waterproof paint on all external walls. If it appears after construction: do NOT paint over it. Remove with diluted acid wash, identify and stop the water source, let wall dry completely (takes weeks), then repaint with anti-efflorescence primer first.
